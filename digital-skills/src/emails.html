Copy a template, replace everything in [square brackets] and read it once more before you send. Keep each email to one topic.

Every email: a specific subject · a greeting ("Hi Mel," or "Dear Ms Nguyen,") · why you're writing in the first line · short paragraphs · what you need and by when · "Thanks," or "Kind regards," and your full name.

1. Applying for a job

Subject: Application: [Job title] — [Your name]

Dear [Manager's name / Hiring Manager],

I'd like to apply for the [job title] position advertised on [where you saw it]. I've attached my resume and cover letter.

I'm a [two or three words that describe you] [student / person] with experience in [one or two things from the ad], and I'm available [days and times].

Thank you for considering my application. I'd welcome the chance to talk about the role.

Kind regards,
[Full name]
[Mobile]

Attach your resume as Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf. If there's no separate cover letter, put it in the email body instead.

2. Following up an application

Subject: Following up: [Job title] application — [Your name]

Hi [Name],

I applied for the [job title] position on [date] and wanted to check whether you've had a chance to look at applications yet.

I'm still very interested in the role and I'm available [days and times]. Please let me know if you need anything else from me.

Thanks,
[Full name]
[Mobile]

Wait about a week after applying. Follow up once.

3. Calling in sick

Subject: Sick today — [Your name], [day] [time] shift

Hi [Manager's name],

I'm sorry, but I'm unwell and won't be able to work my shift today ([time]–[time]). [I'll get a medical certificate if you need one.]

[Optional: Priya mentioned she might be able to cover.] I'll let you know how I'm going before my next shift on [day].

Thanks for understanding,
[Full name]

Many workplaces want a phone call, not an email, and as early as possible. Check your workplace's rule and follow it.

4. Swapping a shift

Subject: Shift swap request — [Day date]

Hi [Manager's name],

I'd like to request a shift swap for [day date] ([time]–[time]). I have [reason]. [Name] has agreed to cover the shift, and I can take [their] [day date] shift instead.

Could you please let me know if that's OK by [day]?

Thanks,
[Full name]

Find someone to cover first, and ask with plenty of notice.

5. Asking a question

Subject: Question about [topic]

Hi [Name],

Could you help me with a quick question about [topic]? [One or two sentences of detail: what you've already tried or found.]

[The question, as clearly as you can.]

If it's easier to talk, I'm free [times]. Thanks for your help!

[Full name]

6. Saying thank you

Subject: Thank you — [what it's about]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for [the interview / showing me the till / your help with …] [today / on Friday]. [One specific thing you appreciated or learned.]

[If it was an interview: I'm even more keen on the role now, and I look forward to hearing from you.]

Kind regards,
[Full name]

7. Sending a file someone asked for

Subject: [File name or topic] — as requested

Hi [Name],

As requested, I've attached [what it is]. [One line about anything they should know, e.g. "The totals are on the second tab."]

Let me know if you need anything else.

Thanks,
[Full name]

Before you hit send

☐The subject says what the email is about
☐The file you mentioned is actually attached
☐Names, dates and times are right; spellcheck done
☐You'd be OK with your manager reading it (emails get forwarded)
☐The right people are in To and CC; Reply all only if everyone needs your answer
☐Friendly tone: no ALL CAPS, emojis only for people you know well (if at all)
