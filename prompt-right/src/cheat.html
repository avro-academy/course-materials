The 4-part prompt

TaskWhat exactly do I want?
ContextWhat does it need to know?
FormatHow long? What shape?
AudienceWho will read it?
Write a text message to my café manager asking to swap my Saturday 8am shift because I have an exam. Sam has agreed to cover. Under 80 words, polite and friendly.

Quick test: could a new co-worker who knew nothing do this well from your prompt? If not, add what's missing.

Show, don't describe

Write 3 more captions in the same style as this example:
"[paste example]"
Turn my notes into an update using exactly this layout:
Done this week: …
Next week: …
Help needed: …

Set limits

  • Length: "in 3 bullet points", "under 100 words"
  • Reading level: "plain English a Year 8 student would understand"
  • Avoid: "no jargon", "no exclamation marks"
  • Honesty: "don't make up facts: leave a [gap] if you don't know"
  • Language: "in Australian English"

Role, goal, ask me first

You are an experienced retail manager who hires young people. Tell me the 3 things you'd most want me to improve in my resume summary.

Say why: "shorter, because my manager reads it on their phone".

Before you suggest anything, ask me up to 5 questions you need answered. Ask them one at a time.

Long prompts that stay clear

Summarise the job ad below.
JOB AD:
"""
[paste]
"""
Give me:
1. What the job involves, 3 bullets
2. The skills they want, as a checklist
3. Two interview questions
  • Label pasted text and keep it apart from instructions
  • Number the parts you want back
  • Long document first, question last
  • One job per prompt: outline first, then each section

Follow-ups that work

Shorter"Cut this to half. Keep the main point."
Tone"Friendlier and less formal."
Choices"3 versions: funny, serious, in between."
Why"Explain why you wrote it that way."
One fix"Keep everything, change sentence 2."
Angle"Now from the customer's point of view."

Be specific: not "make it better" but what is wrong and how to change it.

When it gets it wrong

That's not right: [the fact]. Fix that and keep the rest the same.
Check your answer carefully. Is anything possibly wrong, made up or out of date? List what I should double-check.

Start a fresh chat when it repeats a mistake, you change topic, or a long chat gets worse.

Ask neutrally: "What's the evidence for and against…?" not "Isn't it true that…?"

Feedback, not a rewrite

Don't rewrite it. Give me:
1. Two things that work well
2. The 3 most important things to improve, with an example each
3. Spelling or grammar mistakes
Check my email against these rules: [rules]. For each, say yes or no and why.
Give me the strongest arguments AGAINST this.

Patterns for real life

Practise"You play my manager. After 4 or 5 messages, give me feedback."
Resume"Turn these into 4 bullet points with action verbs. Don't add anything I haven't said."
Interview"Ask me one question at a time, give one tip after each answer."
Learn"Explain it with an example about [my life], then check me with 2 questions."
Quiz me"8 questions, one at a time, explain anything I get wrong."
Notes"Table: Task, Who, By when. Put ? instead of guessing."
Ideas"Give me 20 ideas, easy to ambitious, then pick the 3 best and say why."
Decide"Compare in a table, then ask me 3 questions. Don't decide for me."

Check before you trust

  • Facts, dates and numbers
  • Names and quotes
  • Sources: AI can invent books, laws and links
  • Money, health, law, safety: official source or a real person
  • Anything recent
For each fact, how confident are you (high, medium, low) and what source should I check it against?

Open the links. Prefer .gov.au, your school, the company itself. Find a second source.

Use it responsibly

  • Follow your school, TAFE or work AI rules
  • Say when AI helped a lot
  • Never paste private info: yours or anyone's
  • Push back on stereotypes
  • No fake images, voices or messages of real people
