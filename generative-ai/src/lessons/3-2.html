The harms to look for

HarmExample at Brightside
Wrong informationStudy Buddy explains a maths method incorrectly, and a student learns it the wrong way
Harmful contentSomeone tricks the bot into rude, frightening or adult content in front of a 10-year-old
Unfair treatmentThe email sorter marks messages written in non-standard English as complaints more often
Privacy leaksThe bot repeats one family's details to another
Over-relianceStudents copy answers instead of learning

Four layers of protection

No single fix stops every harm, so responsible apps stack several layers. If one misses something, the next can catch it.

  1. The model. Pick one suited to the job, and test it with your own examples. Smaller models are often easier to trick, so the other layers matter more
  2. Safety filters. Check what goes in and what comes out. Hosted AI services usually include content filters; with a local model you can add your own checks, or a small safety model that labels text as safe or unsafe
  3. The system prompt (sometimes called the metaprompt). The instructions the user doesn't see: the bot's job, its limits and how to say no. You'll write one in Module 5
  4. The user experience. Tell people it's AI, show sources, limit what can be typed, add a "report a problem" button and keep a person in charge (Module 7.2)

Measure, then improve

Responsible AI is a loop, not a one-off checklist:

  1. Identify the harms that could happen
  2. Measure how often they happen, using test prompts
  3. Reduce them with the layers above
  4. Operate the app with monitoring, so new problems show up quickly — then go round again
