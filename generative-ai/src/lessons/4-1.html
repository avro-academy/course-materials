Prompting when you're the builder

When you chat with AI, you can fix a weak prompt with a follow-up. When you build an app, the prompt is written once, in your code, and used thousands of times by people you'll never meet. It has to work every time. That's prompt engineering: designing and testing prompts until they're reliable.

If you'd like a gentle start with everyday prompting first, our Prompt Right course covers it in depth.

The parts of a prompt

PartWhat it doesExample
InstructionThe task"Write a lesson plan…"
Primary contentThe text to work onThe parent's email, the notes, the student's question
ContextWhat it needs to know"…for a 60-minute small-group lesson, Year 8 maths, fractions"
ExamplesShow what a good answer looks likeTwo emails already sorted correctly (Module 4.2)
FormatThe shape of the answer"Use these headings…", "Reply with JSON"
CuesThe start of the answer, to steer itEnding the prompt with "Category:"

Messages and roles

Chat models take a list of messages, each with a role:

  • system — your instructions for the whole conversation: the bot's job, rules and tone. The user doesn't see it
  • user — what the person typed
  • assistant — what the model replied earlier

The system prompt is where most of an app's personality and safety rules live. A good one is specific:

Vague: You are a helpful tutor.

Specific: You are Study Buddy, a homework helper for Brightside Tutoring students in Years 3 to 12. Don't give final answers to homework; give a hint or the first step, then ask the student to try. Keep replies to 4 sentences. Use simple words and Australian spelling. If you aren't sure something is right, say so.

Good habits

  • Be specific about length, audience and format
  • Put instructions first, then the content, and mark where the content starts and ends (with """ or headings)
  • Say what to do, not only what not to do
  • Give it a way out: "If the notes don't say, reply 'I don't know'"
  • Test with lots of inputs, including strange ones — then change one thing at a time
  • Keep prompts in your code, with version control, like any other code
