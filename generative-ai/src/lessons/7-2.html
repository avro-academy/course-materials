People decide whether to trust it

An AI app can be clever and still fail if people don't trust it — or trust it too much. Good design helps people know when to rely on it and when to check.

Start with the user's need

Before adding AI, ask: what is the person trying to do, and is AI the best way? A parent who wants the term dates may be happier with a simple page than a chatbot. Good AI features are:

  • Useful — they solve a real problem, better than the old way
  • Reliable — they work most of the time, and fail gently
  • Accessible — they work for people with different abilities and devices
  • Pleasant — fast enough, and not annoying

Design for collaboration and feedback

  • Let people edit AI drafts before they're used: tutors edit the lesson plan, they don't just print it
  • Suggest, don't act, for anything important
  • Ask for feedback with thumbs up / down and an optional comment; use it to improve prompts and tests
  • Make it easy to undo and to start again
  • Show progress: streaming text word by word (Module 5.2) feels faster than a spinner

Don't forget accessibility

Use clear language, good colour contrast and labels that screen readers can read. Let people use a keyboard. Don't make the AI the only way to get important information.
