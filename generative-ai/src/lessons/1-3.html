Where it shines

Generative AI is strongest when the job is about language and patterns, and a person checks the result:

  • First drafts — emails, reports, lesson plans, product descriptions
  • Rewriting — shorter, simpler, more formal, in another language
  • Summarising — long documents, meeting notes, a page of reviews
  • Explaining — a topic at the right level, with examples, as many times as you need
  • Sorting and pulling out details — "Which of these 200 emails are complaints?", "Get the name and phone number out of this form"
  • Code — writing, explaining and fixing small programs

Why it hallucinates

Remember Module 1.2: the model predicts likely text. A real-sounding book title, a plausible statistic and a correct fact all look the same to it. It has no built-in sense of "I don't actually know this". Newer models are better at saying "I'm not sure", but none are perfect.

That's why the apps you'll build later in this course give the model the facts it needs (Module 6), keep the job narrow, and are tested before anyone relies on them.

A simple rule

Use AI for the first 80% of a job. Use your own judgement for the last 20% — and always for anything that goes to a customer, a teacher or a boss.

Try it

  1. Ask an AI chat tool: "Give me three books about running a small tutoring business, with authors and years."
  2. Search for each one online. How many are real, with the right author and year?
  3. Then ask it: "How many letter r's are in 'strawberry raspberry'?" Count them yourself.
