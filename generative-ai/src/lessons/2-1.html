Not one AI, but many models

"AI" in the news usually means one of hundreds of models, each trained for different jobs. Picking the right one is one of the first decisions when you build something.

Base models and chat models

A base model (also called a foundation model) has only learned to continue text. Ask it a question and it might reply with three more questions, because that's what often follows a question on the web. A chat or instruct model has had extra training to follow instructions and hold a conversation. For apps you nearly always want the chat or instruct version; names often end in -instruct or -chat.

ExamplesOpenAI's GPT, Anthropic's Claude, Google's GeminiMeta's Llama, Alibaba's Qwen, Google's Gemma, Mistral, Microsoft's Phi

PrivacyYour text goes to the providerCan stay entirely on your computer
ControlThe provider can change or retire a modelYou keep the exact version, and can fine-tune it (Module 8)

"Open-weight" means you can download the trained model. It doesn't always mean you can use it for anything: each model has its own licence. Some, like Qwen and Mistral's small models, use the Apache 2.0 licence; Llama and Gemma have their own terms with some limits. Read the licence before you build a business on one.

So which one?

Ask four questions:

  1. What's the job? Text, search, images? Simple sorting or hard reasoning?
  2. How private is the data? Student details might be a reason to keep things local
  3. How fast and how cheap must it be? A smaller model is faster and cheaper
  4. How good is good enough? Test with your own examples (Module 2.4) rather than trusting rankings

In this course you'll use small open models, because they're free and private. Everything you learn works the same way with the big hosted ones.
