Why this comes before the code

An AI app can do real harm: a homework helper that gives a student wrong information, a sorter that treats some parents' emails unfairly, a chatbot that leaks someone's phone number. Once an app is in front of people, mistakes spread fast and damage trust. So responsible builders think about harm before they build, not after a complaint.

Six principles

Many organisations — governments, universities, tech companies — have published principles for responsible AI. Most of them cover the same six ideas. Australia's own AI Ethics Principles cover similar ground.

FairnessTreat everyone equally wellExplains just as well for a student with weaker English
Reliability and safetyWorks as expected and doesn't cause harmTested before students use it; says "I'm not sure" when it isn't

Students and young people

Study Buddy is for school students, so take extra care:

  • Check the age rules of any AI service you use. Many hosted AI services are for adults only, or need a parent's permission
  • Collect as little personal information as you can
  • Keep a person involved: Study Buddy helps, but tutors teach
  • Support learning, don't replace it — that's why Study Buddy gives hints instead of answers

The question to keep asking

Who could this hurt, and how would we know?
