From words to pictures

Image models make a picture from a text description, called a prompt. Most of today's image models use a method called diffusion:

  1. In training, the model sees millions of images with captions, and learns to remove "noise" (random static) from blurred-out versions of them
  2. To make a new image, it starts from pure noise and removes it step by step, guided by your prompt, until a picture appears

That's why the same prompt gives a different image each time: it starts from different noise.

Where you can try it free

Several AI chat tools can make images in their free plans (with daily limits), and so do many design apps. Check the age rules and terms first: some are adults only, and some don't let you use the images commercially. Running image models on your own computer is possible, but needs a powerful graphics card, so this lesson uses a web tool.

Writing image prompts

PartExample
SubjectThree high school students working on maths together at a table
SettingIn a bright, modern tutoring room with a whiteboard
StyleFlat vector illustration / watercolour / realistic photo
Mood and colourWarm, friendly; teal and orange
LayoutWide banner, space on the left for text
Weak: students studying

Strong: Flat vector illustration of three high school students of different backgrounds working on maths together at a table in a bright tutoring room with a whiteboard. Warm, friendly mood, teal and orange colours. Wide banner layout with empty space on the left.

Image models are often bad at text inside the image (spelling comes out wrong), hands, and exact counts. Add words yourself afterwards in a design tool.

Using images responsibly

  • Don't make images of real people without their permission — especially children — and never anything that could embarrass or mislead
  • Say it's AI-made when it matters, for example in an advertisement
  • Watch for stereotypes: "a doctor" or "a nurse" may come out with one gender or skin colour; describe who you want
  • Respect artists: don't ask for a picture "in the style of" a living artist for commercial work
  • Check the licence of the tool before using images for a business

Apps that let users make images add a system prompt (Module 3.2) with rules like "No images of real people. No violence. Keep every image suitable for children."

Try it

  1. In a free image tool, make a banner for Brightside's holiday maths workshop with the weak prompt, then the strong one.
  2. Change one part at a time (style, then colours) and compare.
  3. Look for problems: wrong text, odd hands, stereotypes. How would you fix them?
