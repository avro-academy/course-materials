Size is measured in parameters

A model's size is the number of parameters (the numbers it learned in training), written like 3B for 3 billion. As a rough guide:

SizeRuns onGood at
1B to 4B ("small language models")Most laptops with 8 GB of memory, and phonesSimple chat, sorting, summarising, pulling out details, answering from notes you give it
7B to 14BLaptops with 16 GB or moreBetter writing and reasoning
30B to 70BPowerful desktops with a big graphics card, or serversClose to the hosted models for many jobs
Hundreds of billionsData centresThe hardest reasoning, coding and writing

Why small models matter

Small language models (SLMs) are trained more carefully on better-chosen data, so a 3B model today can beat much bigger models from a few years ago. They're useful when you need:

  • Privacy — nothing leaves the device
  • Speed and low cost — no internet round trip, no per-token bill
  • Offline use — a classroom app that works without Wi-Fi
  • A narrow job done well — like sorting messages, where a giant model is overkill

The trade-off: they know less, make more mistakes on hard questions, and need clearer prompts.

Quantisation: making models fit

Each parameter is normally stored with lots of decimal places. Quantisation stores them with fewer, making the model about a quarter of the size, with only a small drop in quality. That's how a 3B model fits in about 2 GB. The models Ollama downloads are already quantised.
