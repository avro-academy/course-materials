Searching by meaning

A normal search matches words. Ask "Can I get my money back?" and it won't find a page titled "Refunds", because none of the words match. Semantic search matches meaning, so it finds the refunds page anyway.

It works with embeddings: an embedding model turns a piece of text into a long list of numbers that describes what it means. Texts with similar meanings get similar lists of numbers, even if they share no words.

See it for real

module-6/embed_demo.py

"""Turn sentences into embeddings and see which ones are close in meaning."""
import math

import ollama

EMBED_MODEL = "nomic-embed-text"


def cosine(a, b):
    dot = sum(x * y for x, y in zip(a, b))
    return dot / (math.sqrt(sum(x * x for x in a)) * math.sqrt(sum(y * y for y in b)))


sentences = [
    "How much does a lesson cost?",
    "What are your prices?",
    "Is my child safe with your tutors?",
    "The cat sat on the mat.",
]

vectors = ollama.embed(model=EMBED_MODEL, input=sentences)["embeddings"]
print(f"Each embedding has {len(vectors[0])} numbers. The first five: {[round(x, 3) for x in vectors[0][:5]]}")
print()

for i, sentence in enumerate(sentences[1:], start=1):
    print(f"{cosine(vectors[0], vectors[i]):.3f}  '{sentences[0]}' vs '{sentence}'")
  • ollama.embed sends a list of texts to the embedding model and returns one embedding (a list of numbers) for each
  • cosine measures how closely two embeddings point the same way (next section)

Result:

Each embedding has 768 numbers. The first five: [-0.011, 0.084, -0.165, -0.039, 0.016]

0.538  'How much does a lesson cost?' vs 'What are your prices?'
0.499  'How much does a lesson cost?' vs 'Is my child safe with your tutors?'
0.381  'How much does a lesson cost?' vs 'The cat sat on the mat.'

"How much does a lesson cost?" and "What are your prices?" share no important words, but they score highest. The cat sentence scores lowest. The tutoring safety question sits in between: it's about the same business, but a different topic.

You can't read meaning from the numbers themselves; what matters is how embeddings compare with each other.

Cosine similarity

Think of each embedding as an arrow pointing in a direction, in 768 dimensions instead of the usual 2 or 3. Cosine similarity measures the angle between two arrows:

  • Close to 1: pointing the same way, similar meaning
  • Near 0: unrelated

The scores depend on the model, so don't compare them between models; compare them with each other. With this model, 0.54 against 0.38 is a big difference.

What embeddings are used for

  • Recommendations: "students who liked this worksheet also…"
  • Grouping similar messages, and spotting duplicates
