A tool for tutors

Brightside's tutors spend hours writing lesson plans. A template app lets a tutor type four details and get a plan in Brightside's standard layout every time. The tutor then edits it: the app writes the first draft, the person decides.

module-5/lesson_plan.py

"""A lesson-plan generator: a prompt template filled in from what the tutor types."""
import ollama

MODEL = "llama3.2"

TEMPLATE = """You are helping a tutor at Brightside Tutoring plan a lesson.

Write a lesson plan for a {minutes}-minute small-group lesson.
Subject: {subject}
Year level: {year}
Topic: {topic}

Use exactly these headings: Goal, Warm-up, Teach, Practice, Exit question, Homework.
Give times in minutes for Warm-up, Teach and Practice, adding up to {minutes} minutes or less.
Keep the whole plan under 200 words. Use Australian spelling."""

subject = input("Subject: ")
year = input("Year level: ")
topic = input("Topic: ")
minutes = input("Lesson length in minutes [60]: ") or "60"

prompt = TEMPLATE.format(subject=subject, year=year, topic=topic, minutes=minutes)

stream = ollama.chat(model=MODEL, messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}],
                     options={"temperature": 0.4}, stream=True)
print()
for chunk in stream:
    print(chunk["message"]["content"], end="", flush=True)
print()

What's new:

  • The template holds everything that stays the same: the job, the exact headings, a time rule, a length limit and the spelling. TEMPLATE.format(...) fills in the blanks
  • or "60" gives a default if the tutor just presses Enter
  • Temperature 0.4: some variety in the writing, but not wild
  • stream=True returns the reply in small pieces as the model writes them. Printing each piece with end="" and flush=True makes the words appear one after another, so the tutor isn't staring at a blank screen

Topic: Solving two-step equations
Lesson length in minutes [60]: 60

Lesson Plan: Solving Two-Step Equations

**Goal**: Students will be able to solve two-step equations using addition and subtraction.

**Warm-up (5 minutes)**: Quick review of one-step equations, asking students to solve simple equations on the board.

**Teach (20 minutes)**: Introduce the concept of two-step equations, using visual aids to illustrate the process. Provide examples of equations with addition and subtraction, such as 2x + 5 = 11. Emphasise the importance of following the order of operations.

**Practice (25 minutes)**: Distribute worksheets with two-step equations. Have students work in pairs to solve the equations, circulating around the room to provide guidance and feedback.

**Exit question (5 minutes)**: Ask students to solve a simple two-step equation on the board, such as 3x - 2 = 7. This will reinforce their understanding and provide a quick assessment of their progress.

**Homework**: Assign a set of practice questions for students to complete at home, focusing on solving two-step equations with addition and subtraction.

A tutor would still change things: two-step equations like 2x + 5 = 11 also need division, not only "addition and subtraction", and the homework is vague. That's the point of keeping a person in charge. The words "Lesson Plan:" at the top weren't asked for either — small models often add a little extra.

Why templates beat a chat box

  • Consistent results: every plan has the same layout, so tutors know where to look
  • Tested once, used often: you improve the template, and every tutor benefits
  • Safer: users fill in short fields instead of writing their own instructions

Try it

  1. Run it for a different subject and year. Check each rule in the table.
  2. Add a rule to the template: "In Teach, include one worked example with every step." Run it again.
  3. Make a second template for an end-of-term report comment, with the student's name, subject and two strengths as blanks.
