Don't trust the rankings, test

Websites rank models on general tests, but what matters is how a model does on your job. A fair comparison has:

  • The same questions for every model, taken from the real job
  • A known good answer for each, written before you look at the results
  • A score for each answer, given the same way every time
  • Speed and, for hosted models, cost

Run the comparison

Open module-2/compare_models.py:

module-2/compare_models.py

"""Ask several models the same questions and save the answers to a CSV file to compare."""
import csv
import time

import ollama

MODELS = ["llama3.2", "qwen3:4b-instruct", "gemma3:4b"]

QUESTIONS = [
    "Explain what a fraction is to a Year 4 student, in two sentences.",
    "Two brothers each take 10 small-group lessons at $45 a lesson. Siblings get 10% off. What is the total?",
    "Write a one-line reminder text for a parent about tomorrow's 4 pm maths lesson.",
]

with open("model-results.csv", "w", newline="", encoding="utf-8") as f:
    writer = csv.writer(f)
    writer.writerow(["model", "question", "answer", "seconds"])
    for model in MODELS:
        for question in QUESTIONS:
            start = time.time()
            response = ollama.chat(model=model, messages=[{"role": "user", "content": question}])
            seconds = round(time.time() - start, 1)
            answer = response["message"]["content"].strip()
            writer.writerow([model, question, answer, seconds])
            print(f"{model} ({seconds}s): {answer[:70]}...")

print("Saved model-results.csv")
  • ollama.chat sends a list of messages to a model and waits for the reply. You'll learn it properly in Module 5
  • Each answer goes into a row of a CSV file, which opens in any spreadsheet app
  • time.time() before and after measures how long each answer took

Read the answers

Open model-results.csv. The interesting one is the maths question. The right answer is 2 × 10 × $45 = $900, less 10% = $810.

llama3.2 (the end of its answer):

Each brother takes 10 lessons at $45 per lesson, so the total cost before discount is:

10 lessons x $45 per lesson = $450

Since the siblings get 10% off, we need to calculate the discount amount:

$450 x 10% = $450 x 0.10 = $45

Now, we subtract the discount from the original cost:

$450 - $45 = $405

So, the total cost with the 10% discount is $405.

Clear, confident and wrong: it forgot the second brother. qwen3:4b-instruct and gemma3:4b both got $810:

So, apply 10% discount to the total:

$$
10\% \text{ of } \$900 = 0.10 \times 900 = \$90
$$

Subtract that discount:

$$
\$900 - \$90 = \$810
$$

✅ **Final Answer: \$810**.

Notice the $$ and \text: that's LaTeX, a way of writing maths that some apps turn into neat formulas. In a plain text message to a parent it would look messy. That's worth a note on your test sheet too.

The other questions show differences in style: Qwen and Gemma added emoji to the reminder; llama3.2 added "don't forget to pick them up after school", which nobody asked for. Would Priya want either?

Three questions aren't enough to decide, but even this small test shows something useful: for anything with sums, don't rely on llama3.2 — or do the maths in code (Module 4.2). Real tests use 20 to 50 questions.

Write test questions that can only be read one way

An earlier version of the maths question said "How much do 10 lessons cost for two brothers with a 10% discount on both?" Two of the three models read it as 10 lessons shared, not 10 each. If people could read your question two ways, so can a model, and your test measures the wording, not the model.
