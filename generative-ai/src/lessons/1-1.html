AI that makes things

Most software you use follows rules a person wrote: press this button, that happens. Generative AI is different. It makes new things — a paragraph, a picture, a piece of code, a voice — that didn't exist until you asked. Chat tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot are the best-known examples, but the same ideas sit inside email apps, search engines, photo editors and customer help pages.

In this course you'll learn what's going on inside these tools, how to use them safely, and how to build small AI apps of your own in Python, with free models that run on your own computer.

How we got here

People have tried to make computers talk for over 60 years. Each step solved a problem the last one couldn't.

WhenWhat happenedIts limit
1960sRule-based chatbots like ELIZA matched keywords and replied from a scriptAnything outside the script broke them
1990sStatistical models learned patterns from examples instead of hand-written rules — the start of machine learningGood at narrow jobs, like spam filters, but couldn't hold a conversation
2010sNeural networks trained on far more data, using faster hardware, powered voice assistants like SiriStill struggled to keep track of long sentences
2017Researchers published the transformer, a design that looks at every word in a passage at once and works out which ones matter to each otherNeeded huge amounts of text and computing power to shine
2020sLarge language models (LLMs), transformers trained on a large share of the public internet, can write, summarise, translate and answer questions on almost any topicThey can still be confidently wrong — Module 1.3

The big change wasn't one clever trick. It was the transformer design plus enormous amounts of text plus the computing power to train on it.

What generative AI can make

  • Text — emails, summaries, lesson plans, answers to questions, translations
  • Code — programs, spreadsheet formulas, website pages
  • Images — pictures from a description (Module 7)
  • Audio and video — speech in a natural voice, music, short video clips

Some models handle more than one of these at once: you can show them a photo and ask a question about it. These are called multimodal models.

Why it matters for you

Businesses of every size now use generative AI to answer customer questions, write first drafts and search their own documents. People who understand what it can and can't do — and who can build simple tools with it — are in demand in almost every kind of job, not only in IT.
