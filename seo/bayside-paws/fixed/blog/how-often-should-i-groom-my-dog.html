How often should I groom my dog?

Most dogs need a professional groom every 4 to 12 weeks, depending on their coat. Curly and long coats need grooming most often; short coats the least. Here's a simple guide.

Coat typeExample breedsProfessional groomBrushing at home
Curly or woolSpoodle, cavoodle, poodleEvery 4–6 weeksDaily
Long and silkyShih tzu, MalteseEvery 4–8 weeksDaily
Double coatGolden retriever, huskyEvery 8–12 weeks2–3 times a week
Short and smoothStaffy, beagleEvery 8–12 weeksWeekly

Why regular grooming matters

Grooming isn't just about looks. Regular brushing and professional grooms stop painful mats forming close to the skin, keep nails from growing into the paw pads, and give us a chance to spot skin problems, lumps or ear infections early. Dogs that are groomed on a regular schedule also find the salon less stressful, because it becomes familiar.

Between grooms: what to do at home

  • Brush curly and long coats every day with a slicker brush, working down to the skin.
  • Check ears weekly for redness or a bad smell.
  • Bath at home no more than every 2 to 4 weeks, with a dog shampoo; human shampoo dries out their skin.

Signs your dog needs a groom sooner

  • Knots or mats behind the ears or under the legs
  • Nails clicking on the floor
  • A doggy smell soon after a bath
