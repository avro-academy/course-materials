Cut along the dashed lines, or keep the sheet by your mirror. Say each pair slowly, then at normal speed. Ask a friend to point to one word while you say it: can they tell which you meant?

Short i vs long ee

Lips: spread a little and hold longer for "ee".

ship – sheep · fill – feel · live – leave · bit – beat · sit – seat · chip – cheap

Please fill the form if you feel unwell.

Short oo vs long oo

Lips: rounded and pushed forward for long "oo".

full – fool · pull – pool · look – Luke · could – cooed

The full order is for Luke.

cut/cart · bed/bad

Hold "cart" and "heart" longer. Open your jaw more for "bad" and "pan".

cut – cart · hut – heart · bed – bad · pen – pan · met – mat

Park the cart before you cut the cake. Put the pan next to the pen.

Open your mouth

Let your jaw drop on every word.

car · park · start · half · laugh · dance

Park the car and start the dance. Have a great day!

p, b, m (lips together)

Close your lips fully, especially at the end of words.

stop · job · team · help · club · cup – cub · cap – cab

A proper copper coffee pot.

v vs w

v: top teeth on bottom lip, buzz. w: rounded lips open fast, no teeth.

vest – west · vine – wine · veil – whale · verse – worse · vet – wet

Vincent's van went west while Wendy waved very wildly.

th (voiceless)

Tongue tip lightly between the teeth, blow air. No buzz.

think – tink · three – free · thanks · Thursday · thirty · bath · month

Thirty-three thirsty thinkers thought through Thursday.

th (voiced)

Same tongue position, add your voice (buzz).

this – dis · that – dat · they – day · the · mother · together · with

They brought the other one together with this.

t and d (tongue tip up)

A quick, clean tap on the ridge behind your top teeth. Keep final t and d.

just · next · eight – eighty · told · paid · sent

Just ask the next guest to wait at the front. I need it by eight, not eighty.

s vs sh

s: lips spread, thin hiss. sh: lips pushed forward, wider "shhh".

sip – ship · sell – shell · seat – sheet · sue – shoe · save – shave

She sells seashells by the seashore. Six shoes sit on the shop shelf.

R vs L

L: tongue tip touches the ridge. R: tip pulls back, touches nothing; lips slightly rounded.

right – light · rice – lice · road – load · read – lead · fry – fly · collect – correct

Red leather, yellow leather.

-ed endings

t: walked, helped, finished, stopped
d: played, cleaned, called, served
id: wanted, needed, started, added

I walked to work, cleaned the tables and started the till.

-s endings

s: cups, snacks, shifts, chips
z: rolls, orders, bags, coffees, teas
iz: buses, sizes, dishes, sandwiches, changes

Two coffees, three teas and four sandwiches.

-ing endings

Finish with "ng" — the back of the tongue lifts. Don't drop it to "in'".

working · serving · cleaning · morning · nothing · something

I'm working this morning and cleaning this evening.

Clusters: start of word

Start straight on the first sound: sss-treet, not "es-treet".

street · spring · school · splash · screen · please · clean · crowd · fridge · green

Six slippery snails slid slowly seaward.

Clusters: end of word

Build up: tex → text → texts · mon → month → months.

texts · lists · asked · months · clothes · helped · tasks · desks · fifths · prompts

She asked for the lists of tasks for both months.

Numbers

Stress the end of -teen, the start of -ty. Confirm with digits.

thirTEEN – THIRty · fifTEEN – FIFty · eighTEEN – EIGHty

0412 / 345 / 678 — "So that's 0412 345 678?"

Warm-up twisters

Slow and accurate first, then faster.

Red lorry, yellow lorry.
Unique New York, unique New York.
Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.

Workplace twisters

Fresh flat whites for five fussy friends.
The sixth customer's crisp chips and three cheese sandwiches.
Please print the price lists for the staff kitchen.

Linking and chunking

turn it on → "tur-ni-ton" · pick it up → "pi-ki-tup" · an hour → "a-nour" · far away → "far-raway"

Your order / for three vegetarian wraps / will be ready / at twelve fifteen.
